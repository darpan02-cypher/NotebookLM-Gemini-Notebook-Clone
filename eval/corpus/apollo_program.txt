The Apollo program was run by NASA from 1961 to 1972 with the goal of landing humans on the Moon. President John F. Kennedy announced the goal in a speech to Congress in May 1961, saying the United States should land a man on the Moon and return him safely to Earth before the end of the decade.

Apollo 11 launched on July 16, 1969, from Kennedy Space Center in Florida atop a Saturn V rocket. The crew was Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin, and Michael Collins. Armstrong and Aldrin landed the lunar module, named Eagle, in the Sea of Tranquility on July 20, 1969. Collins stayed in lunar orbit aboard the command module Columbia.

Neil Armstrong was the first person to step onto the lunar surface. Buzz Aldrin followed about 19 minutes later. The two astronauts spent roughly two and a half hours outside the lunar module, collecting about 21.5 kilograms of lunar rock and soil samples and deploying scientific instruments.

The Saturn V was the rocket that carried Apollo astronauts toward the Moon. It stood 110.6 meters tall and remains the most powerful rocket to have flown successfully. It had three stages: the S-IC first stage, the S-II second stage, and the S-IVB third stage, which fired twice to send the spacecraft toward the Moon.

Apollo 13 launched in April 1970 and was meant to be the third lunar landing. An oxygen tank in the service module exploded about two days into the flight, forcing the crew of Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert, and Fred Haise to abandon the landing. They used the lunar module Aquarius as a lifeboat and returned safely to Earth on April 17, 1970.

The Apollo program had six successful crewed Moon landings: Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17. Apollo 17, in December 1972, was the last. Eugene Cernan was the last person to walk on the Moon. Twelve astronauts in total walked on the lunar surface.

Apollo 1 ended in tragedy on January 27, 1967, when a cabin fire during a launch rehearsal killed astronauts Gus Grissom, Ed White, and Roger Chaffee. The fire led to major redesigns of the spacecraft, including a new hatch and a switch away from a pure oxygen atmosphere on the launch pad.

Apollo 15, 16, and 17 carried the lunar roving vehicle, an electric car that let astronauts travel several kilometers from the lunar module. The total cost of the Apollo program was about 25.4 billion dollars at the time, which is roughly 250 billion dollars in modern currency.
